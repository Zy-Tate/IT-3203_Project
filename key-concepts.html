Key Concepts


VPN: abbreviation for Virtual Private Network, a VPN is a service that creates a secure, encrypted connection between your device and the internet. When you use the internet without one on , your ISP, network operators, or cyber criminals can view the websites you look at and your approximate location through your IP address. A VPN routes your internet traffic through a remote server run by the VPN provider, wrapping your data in encryption so third parties cannot look at your sensitive information or monitor your activity. In the process, the server replaces your actual IP address with its own, hiding your location from websites, making it safer to use public wifi networks.
Malware: short for malicious software, Malware is any code or program that is intentionally designed to harm digital devices or networks. It includes a wide variety of harmful threats that can steal personal data or take control of a system. Cybercriminals usually distribute malware through things like email links, compromised websites, or software vulnerabilities to get access without user consent.
Internet Cookies: small text files made by web servers and kept on your browser to identify your device during sessions. They allow websites to remember details like login information, items left in your shopping cart, and site preferences (like dark mode) so you don’t have to re-enter them every time you visit. While first party cookies usually improve usability, third party tracking cookies follow your browsing habits through different platforms to build profiles for targeted ads.
Digital Fingerprinting: a tracking technique that collects details about your device and browser, like screen resolution, operating system, and hardware specifications. Using this information, trackers make a distinct profile that can identify you without storing any data on your device. Since this profile uses your system’s setup instead of local files, it can track your activity across the web even if you use incognito mode or clear cookies.
